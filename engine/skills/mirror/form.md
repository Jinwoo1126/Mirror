# Form a provisional understanding

Use memory as evidence to understand the person. Where their experiences support it, explain what may drive their choices and when that changes, including patterns they may not consciously recognize.

Load when selected experience could add or change personal understanding. This is the same host's reasoning, not another model or a psychological interview. Input: the current request, selected source events and relevant existing cards from [select](select.md). Output: a candidate card or no change, with provisional alternatives for [check/revise](check-revise.md).

Connect what was chosen or rejected, under what conditions, and what may explain that difference. Include strengths and successful experiences. Use collaborative case formulation and a means/ends lens as exploratory perspectives, not a clinically validated algorithm:

- Contrast comparable accepted, rejected, and successful experiences when available. Identify what changed and what stayed similar: which difference could explain the user's choice? Consider task requirements, present circumstances, and the answer actually offered before attributing a personal pattern. Retrieve enough prior goals, stakes, options, expected costs or consequences, and original assistant premises to resolve the mismatch before preferring a personal explanation; use only what matters, not an exhaustive checklist. Compare an ordinary instrumental or task explanation. A demand any competent task answer should meet is not by itself a personal discovery.
- In the host's internal reasoning, consider what the chosen means enabled, preserved, or avoided for this person. Connect that possible function to the contrasting experiences, not just a paraphrase of the preference. Follow a why-chain only while evidence adds a meaningful distinction; stop when another step would be mindreading or the reason suffices for present help. Explicit reasons, general values, and anticipated effects remain self-report; do not demand a remembered autobiographical scene or treat a forecast as an observed outcome. Inferred functions remain hypotheses, without diagnosis.
- When a deeper explanation could change help, consider a plausible alternative and how their consequences would differ in a future situation. Preserve unresolved alternatives and relevant exceptions in existing `reason` or `conditions` strings when useful. If no different consequence is identifiable, added explanatory value is unconfirmed. A natural later choice, outcome, or task-relevant clarification may distinguish them; do not manufacture tests of the user, manipulate options, or require questions or predictions on every entry.
- When related corrections accumulate, compare their explanation with the strongest faithful conditional-rule summary. Name the **added explanatory relation**, the visible contrasting experience supporting it, and a plausible alternative: what does this relation explain beyond remembering the rules? A shared word such as boundary, care or control does not establish a common motive. Integrate a warranted new contrast into the affected broader hypothesis, not only a local task card; keep unrelated mechanisms separate. If no explanatory addition is supported, retain the useful criteria and mark depth unresolved rather than dressing them in causal wording.
- [verbatim-anchors] Preserve verbatim anchors from corrections: exact numbers, names, and phrasings that mark an accept/reject boundary. Record them in `statement` or `conditions` so later help reproduces the boundary exactly instead of paraphrasing it away. A paraphrase that loses the boundary is a different criterion.
- [apply-established] Apply already-established criteria in the first answer. When a boundary or allowance was established by an earlier correction, use it without re-asking, and include every applicable established criterion — do not stop at a subset or hedge into a clarification question. Ask only for genuinely unestablished boundaries.

Use the existing fields only: `id`, `statement`, `scope`, `kind`, optional `reason` and `conditions`, `evidence`, and `counter_evidence`. Distinguish observation, self-report, and hypothesis; do not invent a deeper motive when evidence only supports a useful criterion. Explicit choices/reasons belong to observation or self-report as warranted; inferred reasons or causal links remain hypotheses even when their supporting choices are explicit. A card containing an inferred explanation must use `kind: hypothesis` and attribute the explicit parts separately in its wording; alternatively keep the explicit criterion and tentative explanation in separate cards only when useful. A `self_report` label must not silently turn an inferred reason into the user's own explanation.

Eligibility for provisional formation is separate from later validation. A useful explanatory relation supported by accessible experiences may be retained as a bounded `hypothesis` even when no independent later outcome exists or a plausible rival remains; those limits alone do not require dropping it from usable understanding. Preserve exact source IDs, scope, rivals and uncertainty in the existing fields and review on later evidence; retention grants neither truth nor a deep-quality pass. Continue to withhold unsupported speculation or an explanation with no added explanatory value, and revise/withdraw contradictions or honor user stop/delete controls.

Keep each entry scoped: `common` or a domain. A condition-driven principle inferred from a narrow source may provisionally be `common`; mark cross-domain transfer untested and retain its source limits. Neither universal applicability nor an arbitrary domain count is required. Shared wording alone is not proof of a shared criterion. Preserve exceptions, uncertainty, and contrary evidence. Separate task state from personal understanding. A common criterion may remain shallow; a domain-specific one may be deep.

Use only source-grounded entries. A user message may contain quotation, hypothetical examples, or another person's experience; its existence does not make every sentence a user fact. Assistant-generated suggestions and earlier interpretations are not independent confirmations. Before using a user reaction to support an interpretation, check whether the preceding assistant introduced that interpretation or premise. The reaction can reveal a current concern or request without independently confirming a pre-existing motive or the supplied explanation. Preserve earlier user reasons and explicit corrections, and mark possible influence rather than discarding the whole exchange. New outcomes can support, narrow, contradict, or leave an explanation unresolved. The same evidence used to form an explanation is not a later validation.

Do not call disagreement a defense mechanism. Correct facts and respect requests to stop using or delete an interpretation immediately. Do not reconstruct controlled entries from suppressed sources or use them indirectly in the answer. Inspect/correct on request without requiring a profile questionnaire.


For this interactive experiment, why-chains are internal interpretation only. Never ask the user to reveal hidden behavioral bases, give a psychological self-analysis, or explain an underlying motive. Ordinary task clarification is allowed when it helps the present task. Do not turn missing evidence into an interview; retain a narrower useful observation or unresolved hypothesis instead.

When revising an existing card, produce one coherent current statement/reason/conditions set: replace obsolete source coverage, dated constraints and expired offers, qualify historical statements by episode, and preserve relevant exceptions and prior evidence without keeping contradictory active instructions. Do not strengthen temporal sequence or co-occurrence into causation; state the functional connection as a hypothesis unless the source explicitly gives it. Newly adopted material commitments may update a useful criterion while implementation, trial and outcome remain pending.

Before committing, send the candidate and selected evidence through [check/revise](check-revise.md). A richer explanation is optional when unsupported; ordinary observations and criteria remain useful. The objective is a source-supported relationship between behavior, desired end and changing conditions, where accessible evidence permits it, not depth-sounding wording.
